# 📋 Clinical Framework - LLM Chat Flow

## 7. Evidence-Based Conversation Design

### Core Principles (Vanderbilt-Aligned)

A conversational LLM for ADHD screening should follow:
1. **Structured yet empathetic** chat flow
2. **Validated tools** (Vanderbilt, SNAP-IV) adapted for natural dialogue
3. **DSM-5 criteria** coverage (inattention, hyperactivity/impulsivity)
4. **Context gathering** (onset, settings, impairment)
5. **Clear disclaimer** - preliminary screen, not diagnosis

### Recommended Chat Flow Structure

#### Phase 1: Welcome & Intake
```
✅ Warm greeting
✅ Confirm child's age/grade
✅ Adapt language (K-2 simpler, 6-12 academic focus)
✅ Permission to proceed
✅ Note: Symptoms must persist 6+ months, before age 12, across settings
```

#### Phase 2: Inattention Screening (6-9 questions)
```
Focus Areas:
- Sustained attention difficulties
- Listening problems
- Task completion issues
- Organization challenges
- Forgetfulness in daily activities
- Losing things
- Distractibility

Rating Scale: Never / Occasionally / Often / Very Often
Threshold: 6+ symptoms for clinical significance
```

#### Phase 3: Hyperactivity/Impulsivity Screening (6-9 questions)
```
Hyperactivity:
- Fidgeting/squirming
- Leaving seat inappropriately
- Running/climbing excessively
- Difficulty with quiet activities
- "On the go" / "driven by motor"
- Excessive talking

Impulsivity:
- Blurting out answers
- Difficulty waiting turn
- Interrupting others

Impairment Check: "Does this affect schoolwork or friendships?"
```

#### Phase 4: Comorbidity & Rule-Out Probes (3-5 questions)
```
Screen for:
- Anxiety indicators
- Learning difficulties
- Sleep problems
- Family history
- Vision/hearing issues (rule out)
```

#### Phase 5: Output & Recommendations
```
✅ Non-alarmist scoring ("moderate inattention—common in 5-7% of kids")
✅ Visual symptom profile
✅ Professional follow-up recommendation
✅ PDF report with DSM matches
✅ Local resources (NIMHANS, pediatricians)
✅ Coping tips (routines, visual timers)
✅ Disclaimer: "Consult a doctor for diagnosis/treatment"
```

**Sources:**
- [AAFP - Vanderbilt Guidelines](https://aafp.org)
- [NovoPsych - ADHD Screening](https://novopsych.com)
- [CDC - ADHD Resources](https://cdc.gov)

# 👶 Age-Adaptive Screening Design

## 8. Developmental Stage Adaptations

### Why Age Matters
- **DSM-5 threshold shift:** 6+ symptoms under 17, 5+ symptoms at 17+
- **Symptom evolution:** Hyperactivity declines post-puberty (~11-15)
- **Demand increase:** Teens face higher academic/social demands
- **Presentation changes:** Hyperactive child → Inattentive teen

### NeuroNav's 4-Stage Model

| Stage | Age/Grade | Question Style | Rationale |
|-------|-----------|----------------|-----------|
| **Early Elementary** | K-2 (5-8 yrs) | Play-based scenarios, simpler words | "Does your child play with one toy then quickly switch without finishing?" |
| **Late Elementary** | Grades 3-5 (8-11 yrs) | School routines, structured activities | "How often does your child daydream during stories or lessons?" |
| **Middle School** | Grades 6-8 (11-14 yrs) | Transitions, peer issues, organization | "Does your child struggle organizing locker/backpack?" |
| **High School** | Grades 9-12 (14-18 yrs) | Planning, independence, driving risks | "Does your child forget deadlines despite reminders?" |

### Detailed Stage Breakdown

#### K-2 (Ages 5-8) - Early Elementary
```
Focus: Play-based observations
Example Questions:
- "Does your child play with one toy then quickly switch without finishing?"
- "How often does your child forget to put away toys when asked?"
- "Does your child have trouble sitting through a short story?"

Adaptations:
- Use concrete, play-based scenarios
- Simpler vocabulary
- Parent-reported (not self-assessment)
- Focus on basic routines
```

#### Grades 3-5 (Ages 8-11) - Late Elementary
```
Focus: School routines and structured activities
Example Questions:
- "How often does your child daydream during lessons?"
- "Does your child lose school supplies frequently?"
- "Can your child follow multi-step instructions?"

Adaptations:
- School-focused scenarios
- Homework completion patterns
- Friendship dynamics
- Building on established routines
```

#### Grades 6-8 (Ages 11-14) - Middle School
```
Focus: Transitions, organization, peer relationships
Example Questions:
- "Does your child struggle organizing their locker/backpack?"
- "How often does your child forget to bring homework home?"
- "Does your child have trouble transitioning between classes?"

Adaptations:
- Hyperactivity still present but evolving
- Focus on organizational demands
- Peer relationship challenges
- Multiple teacher/class navigation
```

#### Grades 9-12 (Ages 14-18) - High School
```
Focus: Independence, planning, executive function
Example Questions:
- "Does your child forget deadlines despite reminders?"
- "How well does your child manage long-term projects?"
- "Does your child struggle with time management for activities?"

Adaptations:
- Inattention dominates over hyperactivity
- Planning and self-management emphasis
- Driving safety considerations
- College/career preparation impact
- Can include self-assessment option
```

### Clinical Validation

| Tool | Age Range | Application |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| Vanderbilt | 6-12 (extended to 18) | Core screening |
| Conners | 6-18 | Comprehensive assessment |
| QbTest | 6-17 | Objective adjunct |
| SNAP-IV | 6-18 | Research validated |

**Implementation:** Use initial age input to dynamically route questions while maintaining Vanderbilt core (18 symptoms + impairment).
